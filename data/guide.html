1. What is it about?

Wallachia, 1456: the land is split into twelve counties, most of them held by the Ottoman army. As Vlad Dracula you lead the resistance and win the country back, one county at a time.

The game has two layers, and you switch between them all the time:

  • The Council is the turn-based strategy map. One turn is one week. Here you manage resources, train troops, build fortifications and send armies into neighbouring counties.
  • Battles run in real time. Whenever you attack or are attacked, you fight on the map of that county and command every soldier yourself.

2. Getting started

The main menu offers:

EngageStart a game. If one is already running, you can continue it, start over or go back.
Load / SaveLoad or save a game. Pick a slot, type a name when saving, confirm with Enter, cancel with Esc.
IntroductionPlays the intro video again. Any key stops it.
OptionsEffects and music volume, brightness/colour/contrast, game speed and scroll speed. D resets everything.
ExitQuits the game. Always quit this way rather than with Alt+F4.
The game has a built-in help screen listing every shortcut: F1 or Ctrl+H (or click the help box at the bottom of the side panel). Scroll with the arrow keys and Page Up/Down, Esc closes it.

3. The Council (strategy map)

The map

Counties shaded brown belong to the enemy, the others are yours. One county is always selected (highlighted), and every Council command applies to it. Select one with a click or the arrow keys. Double-click one of your own counties (or press I) to open its battlefield: that lets you position your troops before an attack or look at the terrain. Blue arrows show your troop movements.

Resources

At the bottom centre you see five values, each with the current stock and, below it, the change per week:

GoldPays for training, building and research.
GrainEvery soldier eats one unit per week. If the upkeep runs out, troops desert.
Building materialNeeded for constructions.
RecruitsMen available to be trained as new troops.
ArmyYour total military strength.

Click a resource picture to show or hide a history graph.

The tabs on the left

TrainingTrain troops. The list shows cost, duration in weeks and combat values (damage, accuracy, armour, stamina, speed, range). New troops appear in the selected county. What is available depends on your armament level.
MovementsShows the troops in the selected county. Click troops, then click a neighbouring county (the cursor turns into a sword there). Your own county = relocation, an enemy county = attack. Until the week ends you can undo any movement by clicking the troop again. Space selects all or none, Backspace inverts the selection.
ConstructionsRepair, upgrade or build bridges, towers, fortifications and strongholds. Only one construction job per county at a time. Clicking the hourglass cancels the job.
InformationResources, troops and buildings of the selected county. For enemy counties you need spies there.
AdministrationResearch in two areas: Armament (better and new units) and Architecture (better buildings). Expensive and slow, but essential.

The seal: ending the week

When all orders are given, click the red seal at the bottom right. The week ends, all orders are carried out, and you are told what happened. Wounded soldiers recover a little every week. If a fight breaks out, the game switches to the battlefield.

4. Battles

Sight and exploration

In foreign counties the map starts out black. Bright areas are what your soldiers see right now, dark areas were explored before but show no enemies. In your own counties (or once spies have delivered a map) there is no black. On the first attack the overview map shows your target; after that, further targets appear in order of importance. Border posts made of crossed planks mark the crossings to neighbouring counties: good spots for guards.

Commanding with the mouse

SelectLeft-click a soldier, or drag a frame around several. Shift adds, Ctrl inverts.
DeselectRight-click.
MoveLeft-click an empty spot.
AttackLeft-click an enemy. The attackers leave their formation.
Scroll the viewMouse to the screen edge, the arrow keys, or hold Alt and move the mouse. Or click and drag on the overview map at the top left.
Zoom NEWMouse wheel: forward to zoom in, back for a wider view (7 levels). The point under the cursor stays in place.
GatesDeselect everyone first (right-click), then click the gate to open or close it, as long as nobody is in the way.

The green bars under selected soldiers show their health.

Commands in the side panel

Every command applies to the selected soldiers. The shortcut is the first letter of the command.

CommandKeyEffect
WalkWThen click the destination.
AttackAThen click an enemy. Without an enemy at the target it becomes a march.
StopSHalt immediately and stay put.
GuardGClick a spot. The soldiers stay there, even when attacked.
PatrolPClick a spot. The soldiers go back and forth between it and their starting point.
FleeFRun away in the least threatened direction.
ManoeuvreMSet a series of waypoints: M and a click for each, Backspace removes the last one, Enter starts.
LeaveLThe whole army retreats (confirm with Y/N). You usually lose soldiers doing this.
Council–Back to the Council (only when you opened the battlefield from the Council).

Walk mode

Space (or the "Formation walk" button) switches between two ways of walking: formation keeps the soldiers' arrangement, good for long distances; assemble sends everyone straight to the clicked point, usually better in combat.

Behaviour

Three buttons set how soldiers behave when you are not commanding them directly:

Coward (yellow dot)Flees at the first damage.
Normal (blue dot)Default: flees only when badly hurt.
Berserk (red dot)Attacks every enemy in sight and fights to the death; a bit more attack, a bit less defence.

5. Units

SwordsmenCheap foot soldiers, agile even in tight spaces. Trained and moved in groups of five in the Council.
CrossbowmenRanged fighters, also in groups of five. Keep riders at a distance; ideal on towers and walls.
KnightFast, strong riders. Their upkeep costs as much as five swordsmen.
JuggernautA cheap battering ram that breaks gates but not walls. Essential until catapults are available.
CatapultDestroys walls and gates and hurls rocks at troops.
CannonLike the catapult, but faster, more accurate and quicker to fire. Early cannons can explode: keep your own men away.
SpiesNever appear on the battlefield. Send them into an enemy county; after a while they report resources, troops, buildings and even a map. If all of them are caught, the knowledge is lost.

Ranged units hit much better when the target is closer than half their range. Soldiers who survive long become veterans with a stronger attack. Badly wounded soldiers walk more slowly.

6. Buildings

Bridges (wood or stone), watchtowers (up to four archers), fortifications and strongholds (bigger walls that soldiers can stand on, optionally with a moat). Large buildings also raise a county's income and help to unmask enemy spies. On walls and towers soldiers see and shoot further; you get onto stronghold walls with a simple click. Houses are impassable: soldiers you want to protect can hide inside.

8. New in this version

  • Large 16:9 battlefield with mouse-wheel zoom in 7 levels; the side panel always keeps its size.
  • Unit-type buttons below the info box: a click selects all living units of that type on the whole map, Shift+click adds them. The number shows how many are still alive.
  • Group buttons 1–9 and 0: a click selects the group, Shift+click adds it, Ctrl+click stores the selection as that group (the same groups as the number keys).
  • Menus and strategy map with blurred side bars, videos without black lines, no CD needed.

9. Tips for beginners

  • Crossbowmen are the best defence: put them on towers and stronghold walls and set them to berserk.
  • Invest in armament early: without catapults and cannons you stand no chance against the Sultan's big strongholds.
  • Move large armies in formation. If the game slows down with many units, select all (Ctrl+A), stop them with S and send them off again in formation.
  • Watch your grain: without supplies your men desert.
  • Send spies before an attack: with their map you see the enemy while you are still approaching.
  • Occupy border posts, bridges and passes where you expect the next attack.
